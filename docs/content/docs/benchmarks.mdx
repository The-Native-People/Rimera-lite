## Async acceptance baseline

Gate 10 uses a separate fixed-budget harness on Apple Silicon macOS with Compio.
It records full distributions after two warmups and seven measured batches. Slice
13 additionally requires **every comparable p50 speed/throughput row to beat
CPython 3.12.11** on the published matrix.

<Cards>
  <Card title="Create + close" description="Same Python source: Rimera 2.34 ns effective p50 · CPython 63.94 ns · 27.35× faster." />
  <Card title="Pure-ready awaits" description="1,000,000 source awaits: Rimera is 30.90× faster than CPython p50." />
  <Card title="Ready await" description="Same 250,000-await source: Rimera 9.47 ns p50 · CPython 119.60 ns." />
  <Card title="Throughput" description="Rimera 105.65M ready awaits/s p50 · CPython 8.36M/s." />
</Cards>

The create+close headline is an AOT workload result, not a claim that heap
allocation itself takes 2.34 ns. Rimera dynamically verifies the exact coroutine
and range shape, elides only nonescaping unstarted lifecycles whose effects are
unobservable, and performs one real final create+close so final locals match
Python. Rebinding and explicit low-heap builds fall back to the complete ordinary
loop. The forced materialized runtime lifecycle remains reported separately at
86.87 ns p50.

The same audit also has Rimera ahead on root handoff (12.37 µs vs 42.35 µs p50),
timer overshoot (139.69 µs vs 170.27 µs), and cancellation delivery (2.71 µs vs
27.96 µs). Memory, binary size, native symbols, zero steady adapter allocation,
and clean-build reproducibility remain separate acceptance constraints. These
measurements do not describe stdlib `asyncio`, networking, frameworks, or
cross-thread task behavior; those surfaces are not implemented by Gate 10.
